How Denver Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Denver homeowner.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Next come appliances. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher when nobody is home, and replace the hoses on a schedule instead of waiting for one to fail. The roof and the ground outside are third. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Going away for more than a few days? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Pueblo Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in Denver. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Denver Homes

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Here is what homeowners around Denver phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Denver can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. In Denver we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.
