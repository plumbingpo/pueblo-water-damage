AC Leak Water Damage in Pueblo: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Nearby Flooring

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.
